The Pocket Lamp review: small, bright and a little pricey

By Riley Chen

RATING : 8.3 / 10

The Pocket Lamp is the kind of gadget that seems unnecessary until the first time the power goes out. It is about the size of a lipstick, clips onto almost anything metal, and throws a surprisingly even pool of light across a desk or a tent floor for most of an evening on a single charge.

I carried one for three weeks: in a backpack, on a bike, and stuck to the side of a fridge during a storm. It survived all of it, though the finish picked up a few scratches along the way, and the clip loosened slightly after a week of heavy use.

Brightness is the headline feature. At its highest setting the lamp is bright enough to read by from across a small room, and the lowest setting is dim enough to leave on as a night light without waking anyone. In between are three steps, cycled with a single button that also locks the lamp so it can't switch on in a bag.

Battery life matched the claim of about six hours on the middle setting, and the lamp charges fully in a little over an hour. There is no battery indicator beyond a small light that turns green when charging is done, which is fine for something this simple but would be nice to have.

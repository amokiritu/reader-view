The office suite moves inside the assistant

Opening the keynote, the chief executive set out a plan to move every office program inside the assistant, so that documents, spreadsheets and slides are created and edited without leaving the conversation that asked for them.

The announcement turns the usual arrangement on its head. For thirty years people opened a program and then asked it for help; now the helper is the program, and the familiar tools are rooms inside it.

Analysts were divided. Some called it the most significant change to office software since the ribbon, while others pointed out that customers have been promised the same thing, under different names, three times before.

All about agents

The second act of the keynote belonged to agents: long-running helpers that keep working after the laptop is closed, file their own progress reports and ask for approval before they spend money.

Each agent is given a budget of tokens, the units in which the work is metered, and administrators can see at a glance which teams are spending the most and on what.

That last feature drew the loudest applause of the morning, which says something about how the first year of these tools has gone for the people who pay the bills.

Governance over trust

Trust was the word the chief executive kept returning to, and not in a triumphant way. He conceded that the inner workings of these systems remain hard to inspect, and that mistakes are certain.

The company answer is a set of controls rather than a promise: every action an agent takes is logged, can be replayed, and can be undone by a person for thirty days.

Whether that is enough will be decided by customers over the coming year, long after the stage lights have been packed away and the keynote forgotten.

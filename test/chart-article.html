Inflation Eases as Food Prices Fall

Inflation eased for a third month in a row as the price of maize flour, cooking oil and fuel came down, the statistics office said on Monday, giving households some relief after two difficult years.

The figures showed prices rising at their slowest pace since early 2024, inside the central bank's target range, though economists cautioned that a poor harvest could quickly reverse the trend.

Fuel prices, which feed into the cost of almost everything else, fell after the regulator cut its monthly maximum prices, the first such cut in more than a year, citing lower import costs.

Traders in the capital's main market said shoppers were buying more than a month ago but still far less than before prices began to climb, and many expected a slow end to the year.

The central bank meets next week and is widely expected to hold its main lending rate, waiting to see whether the slowdown in prices lasts before cutting borrowing costs further.

Economists said the steady fall over four months suggested the worst of the price rises was over, though food prices remain far higher than two years ago and wages have not caught up.

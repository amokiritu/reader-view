A Beginner's Guide to the Home Garden

Why a home garden needs a plan

Most first gardens fail for the same quiet reason: they are planted in a single enthusiastic weekend without much thought about what each plant will need in August. A little planning in late winter saves a great deal of disappointment later in the season.

Start by walking the space at different times of day. Note where the morning sun lands first, where shadows from fences and trees fall by mid-afternoon, and which corners stay damp long after rain. These observations matter more than any seed catalogue.

Reading the light in your yard

Vegetables that produce fruit, such as tomatoes, peppers and squash, generally want six or more hours of direct sun. Leafy greens and herbs like parsley and mint are far more forgiving and will happily grow in the brighter patches of partial shade.

Sketch the area on paper, mark the sunny and shady zones, and only then decide where each bed should go. It is much easier to move a line on paper than to move an established raised bed full of soil.

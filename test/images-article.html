A Weekend Along the Coast Road

The coast road is at its best in early autumn, when the summer crowds have gone home and the light turns soft and golden by mid-afternoon. We drove it over two unhurried days, stopping whenever a view or a bakery demanded attention.

Morning on the cliffs

The first stretch climbs quickly above the water. Lay-bys every few kilometres let you pull over safely, and most have short paths leading to viewpoints where the sea spreads out in every shade of blue.

The wind off the water can be surprisingly cold.

Bring a light jacket even on warm days. The wind off the water can be surprisingly cold at the higher viewpoints, especially before the sun has burned off the morning haze.

Into the hills

By midday the road turns inland through rolling farmland. Hedgerows close in on both sides, and the villages become smaller and quieter, each with a church tower that serves as a landmark long before you arrive.

We stopped for lunch at a farm shop selling cheese, apples and fresh bread, and ate it on a bench overlooking a valley that seemed unchanged for a century.

1/2

The road ends at a small harbour town, where fishing boats still unload in the early morning and the cafés along the quay stay open late into the evening.

We left the next morning, already planning the trip back. The quay was quiet, and the only sound was the gulls arguing over what the boats had left behind.

Coast Roads is an occasional series about the best drives along the shore, from the cliffs to the harbours and everything between.

Next time we will take the inland route, through the moors and the market towns that most visitors only ever see from a train window.
